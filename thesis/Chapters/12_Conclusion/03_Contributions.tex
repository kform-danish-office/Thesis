\section{Original Contributions}
\label{sec:concl-contributions}

The charge-pump PFC principle and planar-magnetic design from earlier DTU
work were integrated into a USB-C power supply. The work covered
the offline input, charge pump, GaN stage, magnetic connections, output
interface, sensing, auxiliaries, and hardware rework during bring-up.

The control firmware uses continuous DMA acquisition and fixed-carrier
burst scheduling to regulate the secondary voltage. Loaded duty adjustment,
startup precharge, and separate no-load behavior address the different
operating conditions. The current-sense interpretation was corrected to
match the installed hardware.

V2 integrated the MCU onto the converter board, requiring adapted timer and
ADC assignments. A flash settings journal, browser commands, and logging
were implemented. Browser calibration and editable reference-load settings
were then added to repeat the duty search for connected test loads. Carrier
frequency and dead time can be set together with output disabled, allowing
switching conditions to be varied without rebuilding the firmware. The four-layer circuit and layout integrate these functions on V2.
The firmware and supporting software passed software checks.
Preliminary powered observations appear in \cref{sec:res-v2-testing};
powered tests of the later adjustable firmware and comparable power and
thermal-endurance measurements are still needed.

The prototype was evaluated for output settings, high-power operation,
input-current quality, initial efficiency, load removal, no-load behavior,
and surface temperature.

A reduced V3 edge-card layout retained secondary rectification while
removing the USB-PD section. It provides a design-level volume reduction
and leaves transformer optimization and thermal testing as the next
development steps.
