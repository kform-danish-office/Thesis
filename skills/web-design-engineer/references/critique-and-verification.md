# Critique and verification

For a critique, inspect the actual artifact and distinguish observed defects from preferences. Review five dimensions:

1. Direction: do choices serve the brief and existing brand?
2. Hierarchy: is the primary action or information apparent at the intended viewing distance?
3. Craft: are alignment, spacing, typography, colors, assets, and motion coherent?
4. Function: can users complete the intended paths and understand feedback?
5. Distinctiveness: does the design express this product without novelty that harms usability?

When scoring is requested, give each dimension a 0–10 assessment with evidence and explain the overall judgment. Do not enforce fixed type ratios, color counts, or font counts independent of context. Report what to keep, fixes ranked by user impact, and a few worthwhile quick improvements when available. Critique the artifact, not its author.

Before delivery, check applicable invariants: project conventions preserved; assets resolve; imports and markup are valid; controls work with keyboard and pointer; focus and feedback are visible; real content fits; responsive layouts avoid unintended clipping; reduced motion is respected; no fabricated proof or unlabeled sample data appears.

For visual checks, inspect screenshots and the runtime at relevant sizes and states. Document bounds alone do not detect hidden overflow in fixed panes. Use screenshots as evidence of visible behavior and source locations as evidence of implementation issues. If checks cannot run, name the gap and limit the claim. Avoid turning an untested suspicion into a confirmed defect.
